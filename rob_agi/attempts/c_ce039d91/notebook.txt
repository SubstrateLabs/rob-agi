Challenge ce039d91: Transform gray (5) cells to blue (1) based on structural importance

Key observations:
1. The transformation preserves the overall structure of the input grid.
2. Isolated gray cells tend to become blue, except on edges.
3. Lines of gray cells are partially transformed, often keeping endpoints gray.
4. Junction points (cells with 3+ gray neighbors) tend to remain gray.
5. 2x2 squares of gray cells are usually fully transformed to blue.
6. The density of gray cells affects the transformation threshold.

What works:
- Calculating structural importance based on neighboring gray cells
- Identifying junction points and 2x2 squares
- Applying a density-based threshold for transformation
- Edge preservation for gray cells

What doesn't work:
- Current handling of lines is not always correct (see test case 2)
- Some cells that should remain gray are being changed to blue

Remaining challenges:
1. Fine-tuning the line detection and transformation algorithm
2. Improving the balance between local and global pattern preservation
3. Handling special cases like X-shapes and T-shapes more accurately

Next steps:
1. Refine the line detection algorithm to better preserve key structural elements
2. Implement a more sophisticated post-processing step to ensure consistency
3. Add specific handling for X-shapes and T-shapes
4. Adjust the threshold calculation to be more sensitive to local patterns

Remember: The goal is to maintain the overall structure while simplifying less important parts of the pattern. Balance between local transformations and global pattern preservation is key.
