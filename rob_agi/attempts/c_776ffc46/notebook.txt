Challenge 776ffc46: Transform blue regions in a grid

Key observations:
1. Blue regions of size 2-4 pixels are transformed to another color (red or green).
2. Blue regions of exactly 5 pixels are transformed only if they form a plus shape.
3. The target color (red or green) is determined by the global prevalence of these colors.
4. Larger blue regions, single blue pixels, and other colors remain unchanged.
5. Gray (5) acts as a border and is not considered part of any region.
6. Only orthogonally adjacent cells are considered part of the same region.
7. All transformations are applied simultaneously.

What we know:
- The transformation rule is consistent across all examples.
- The target color is determined by counting red and green pixels in the entire grid.
- Gray borders do not affect the transformation rules directly, but they define region boundaries.

What doesn't work:
- Transforming based on shapes being "boxed in" by gray borders.
- Changing the target color within a single grid transformation.

Remaining questions:
- How to handle cases where red and green are equally prevalent?
- Are there any special cases for blue regions at the edges of the grid?

Next steps:
1. Implement a more accurate color prevalence counting mechanism.
2. Ensure the flood fill algorithm respects gray borders but doesn't include them in regions.
3. Implement a precise check for the "plus" shape for 5-pixel blue regions.
4. Apply all transformations simultaneously to avoid cascading effects.
