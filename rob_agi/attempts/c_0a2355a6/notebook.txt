Challenge 0a2355a6: Color Assignment Based on Shape Characteristics

Key Observations:
1. The challenge involves transforming a grid with sky blue (8) shapes on a black (0) background into a grid with colored shapes.
2. The color assignment seems to be based on the characteristics of the shapes, primarily their size and possibly their complexity.
3. The largest shape is not always assigned the highest color number.
4. Shapes that are part of the same larger structure (e.g., connected components) should be assigned the same color.

Current Approach:
- Identify distinct shapes using flood-fill algorithm.
- Analyze shapes for size, complexity, and position.
- Rank shapes based primarily on size, with complexity as a secondary factor.
- Assign colors to shapes based on their rank, with the largest shape getting the highest available color.

Issues Identified:
- The current approach incorrectly assigns color 2 to the second largest shape in example_0, when it should be color 1.
- The color assignment logic needs to consider connected components as a single shape.

Next Steps:
1. Modify the shape identification algorithm to treat connected components as a single shape.
2. Refine the color assignment logic to better match the expected output:
   - The largest shape should be assigned color 1 (blue) in most cases.
   - The second largest shape (if distinct) should be assigned color 3 (green).
   - Smaller shapes should be assigned color 2 (red) or 1 (blue) if they're part of a larger structure.
3. Implement a more sophisticated ranking system that considers not just size, but also connectivity and overall structure of the shapes.

Certainties:
- The black (0) background should remain unchanged.
- Each distinct shape or connected component should have a single color.

Unknowns:
- The exact criteria for assigning colors 2 (red) and 4 (yellow) when they appear.
- Whether there are any specific rules for color assignment based on shape position or orientation.

Failed Approaches:

- Strictly ranking shapes by size without considering connectivity leads to incorrect color assignments.
